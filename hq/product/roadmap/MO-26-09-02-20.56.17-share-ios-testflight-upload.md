## Context

Kairos and Evo each maintained their own 500-line `upload-testflight.sh`, and that duplicated
file is where every expensive failure of 2026-09-02 lived. Both projects hit several of them
independently: `-allowProvisioningUpdates` minting an Apple Development certificate on every
run until the shared team (`D495224G8R`) exhausted its account-wide limit and archiving stopped
for both; and, four nights apart, the discovery that a cross-repository reusable workflow
receives none of the caller's environment secrets. Kairos additionally spent ten release runs
on a `plutil` key-path bug, an entitlement check pointed at the wrong artifact, and an upload
that validated one artifact and shipped another. Most of that would not have happened twice if
the implementation were shared.

## Approach

The upload *job* has to stay in the caller — that is the secrets constraint, and it is why
`ios-nightly-build.yml` has `run-upload: false` and exposes `build`/`sha`. A composite action
does not have the problem, because it runs inside the caller's own job. So the implementation
moves to `.github/actions/ios-testflight-upload` and the callers keep only the job.

Configurable through typed inputs: project shape, Apple identifiers, beta-group policy,
`archive-build-settings`, an optional Firebase client plist decoded from a secret for the
archive only, and Sentry. Project-specific assertions go through one hook,
`validate-app-script`, which receives the archived `.app` and none of the credentials — Kairos's
Firebase pinning and Evo's HealthKit purpose strings both fit it.

Kairos's export-verify-upload path becomes the shared default: export an IPA locally, assert its
signature and entitlements, then `asccli builds upload` that same file. `destination: upload`
hands the build to Apple with nothing having inspected it.
