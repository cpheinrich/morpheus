## Context

On 2026-10-06 Chris's Mac hit 100% disk with 4.3 GB free. The weight was iOS Simulator state: 85 GB
of devices (each `Evo QA` clone is 2-4 GB) and 30+ GB of per-worktree build trees, much of it
belonging to simulators nobody had touched in days. Chris asked for a watchdog that shuts down any
simulator open for more than 24 hours without activity, and for it to ship in Morpheus because every
iOS project will want it.

`morpheus qa preview ios` already bounds *its own* device with a four-hour lease, and CI jobs own and
delete their devices. What is left unbounded is everything else: Xcode runs, the Simulator MCP,
hand-booted devices, XCTest clones in `~/Library/Developer/XCTestDevices` after a killed run, and a
preview whose supervisor died. A booted simulator holds RAM and keeps CoreSimulator daemons,
`log` streams and `serve-sim` busy, which is how a machine stays loaded.

## Approach

- `morpheus simulator watchdog run [--dry-run] [--idle-hours N]`: one sweep over the default and the
  XCTest device sets. A booted device is shut down when it has been booted for the threshold
  *and* nothing showed activity inside the threshold. Only `simctl shutdown`: never delete, never
  erase, never `shutdown all` (the rules `qa preview` already keeps).
- **Activity is measured, not guessed.** Probed on a real Mac 2026-10-06: whole-tree mtimes and
  Apple's own AppGroup/PluginKit containers (news, widget timelines, Intelligence embeddings) are
  rewritten every few minutes on a simulator nobody is touching, so counting them would make every
  device look busy forever. The signals used are boot time (the device's `launchd_sim` process
  age), writes inside **user-installed apps'** data containers (found through the bundle ids of
  `Containers/Bundle/Application`), an install, and an explicit heartbeat file that the QA overlay
  writes whenever it forwards a touch or key. Anything that cannot be measured keeps the device up.
- `morpheus simulator watchdog enable|disable|status`: a per-device launchd agent
  (`morpheus.simulator-watchdog`, every 30 minutes) and a preference in
  `~/.morpheus/simulator-watchdog.json`. An explicit device action, never an install side effect,
  matching codebase-memory and auto-update. `self ensure` refreshes an enabled agent so it never
  points at a replaced binary; `doctor` and `qa preview ios start` say when a Mac has no watchdog,
  which is how every iOS project learns of it.
- Docs: a runbook, the `iOS projects` section of AGENTS.md and the `init` template that mirrors it,
  and `architecture.md`.

## Acceptance checks

- Against real simulators on this Mac: `run --dry-run` names the booted devices, their boot ages and
  why each is kept; a device booted past the threshold with no user-app write is shut down by a real
  `run`, and one with a recent write is not.
- `enable` writes a launchd agent that `launchctl print` shows loaded, a sweep fires on its own
  schedule and logs, and `disable` removes it and records the choice so `self ensure` does not
  bring it back.
- Tests pin the boundary (exactly the threshold, one second under), each activity signal, the
  Apple-container exclusion, the unmeasurable-keeps-it-up rule, and that `shutdown` is the only
  simctl write the sweep can issue.
