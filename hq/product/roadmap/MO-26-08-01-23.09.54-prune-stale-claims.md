## Context

Found immediately after shipping the branch-id parser fix (#65), and caused by it in the sense that
fixing one bug made the next one visible: while `listClaims` returned nothing, there were no phantom
claims to notice.

`listClaims` refreshed remote-tracking refs with `git fetch origin --quiet`. **No `--prune`.**
Merging a PR deletes the branch on origin, but a plain fetch leaves the local
`refs/remotes/origin/…` in place — so a shipped item keeps reading as claimed, on every machine that
ever fetched it, forever.

Observed directly: seconds after #65 merged and its branch was deleted, `pm claims` still listed
`MO-26-08-01-23.03.14` as held, and the heartbeat counted it toward the ceiling.

`reconcile` in `ship.ts` had it right — `["fetch", "origin", "--prune", "--quiet"]`. The two
functions ask git the same question and disagreed about the answer, because the argument list was
written twice.

## Why it matters more than a stale table

Same consumer as #65, and the failure is the mirror image. There, claims vanished and the ceiling
undercounted. Here, claims accumulate and the ceiling **overcounts** — every merged item occupies a
lane permanently, so after three merges the heartbeat stops picking work and reports the queue as
full. A dispatcher that quietly stops dispatching is harder to notice than one that misbehaves.

## Approach

One exported `FETCH_PRUNE` in `claim.ts`, used by both readers. The constant is the point: this is
the second bug in two hours caused by the same array or pattern being written twice and drifting,
and the previous fix's whole argument was *one definition, not three*.

## Test plan

- `FETCH_PRUNE` contains `--prune` and `--quiet`.
- A drift guard asserting the literal appears exactly **once** in `claim.ts` — its definition — and
  **never** in `ship.ts`, with both importing the constant.
- Verified against the real remote: before, `pm claims` listed a branch `git ls-remote` no longer
  had; after, the list matches origin exactly.
