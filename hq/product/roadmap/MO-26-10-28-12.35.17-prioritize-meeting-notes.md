## Context

Gemini intake already distils meetings and deduplicates their sources, but the canonical note format
puts context and detailed discussion before the decisions and work a reader is most likely to need.
Chris and Robbie agreed that new notes should lead with an executive summary, key decisions and key
action items, leaving supporting detail later in the record. Guidance alone is too easy to drift, so
the declared format needs a deterministic validation boundary without invalidating historical notes.

## Approach

- Add an opt-in, versioned priority-first note format and keep legacy records valid.
- Validate the first three substantive level-two sections, including non-empty content, while
  ignoring headings inside fenced code blocks.
- Require Gemini discovery version 2 records to declare the new format, and validate their bodies
  before immutable-source projection.
- Update the shared guide, canonical and scaffolded documentation, mirrored skills and architecture
  decision.
- Cover compatibility, ordering, empty-section and fenced-code boundaries with focused tests, then
  typecheck, compile, regenerate indexes and complete independent review.

## Acceptance

- Existing meeting notes without a format declaration still pass validation.
- A `priority-first-v1` note passes only when its first three level-two sections are `Executive
  summary`, `Key decisions` and `Key action items`, in that order, with content in each.
- New Gemini notes use discovery version 2 and the priority-first declaration; version 1 remains
  readable for history.
- Canonical Git ingestion rejects a versioned record whose body does not match its declared format.
- The guide and both provider entrypoints put headline information before detailed notes.
