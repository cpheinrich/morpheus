> **Proposal, for review.** No code. The point is to settle the shape before anything is built,
> because the folder question is the smallest of the decisions in here.

## The intent, restated

Every piece of context about a project that can be digitally captured should be **captured or
referenced in the repo**, so an agent starting cold can reconstruct not just what the state is but
*why*. `.agent/decisions.md` already does this for agent–human exchange. This extends it to
**human–human** exchange: meetings, chat, and whoever the humans are.

## The thing that decides the design, and it is not the folder

`hq/` is organised by **business function** — product, brand, marketing, finance, ops. Meeting notes
and group chat are not a function. They are a **medium**, and they cut across every function: one
meeting covers brand and finance and hiring.

`hq/inbox/` is already the exception that proves this. It sits at the top of `hq/` rather than under
a function, because it is a *record of communication* rather than a body of work. So the category
this proposal needs already exists implicitly, with exactly one member.

**The second decider: raw versus distilled.** §7.5 already splits these and it is the best idea in
the repo's records design:

| Raw | Feeds | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| `inbox-archive/` | `decisions.md` | *What did we decide, and why?* |
| `worklog/` | `learned.md` | *What do we know about how this behaves?* |

A meeting transcript and a chat log are **raw**. They are high volume, low signal, and mostly
irrelevant a week later. If they land as a third thing an agent must read, they make context worse,
not better — the agent reads more and knows less.

**So: capture them raw, and make them feed the distillations that already exist.** A meeting's
decisions promote to `decisions.md`; its action items become roadmap items. The transcript is
evidence, not reading material.

## Proposal

### Layout — `hq/team/`

```
hq/team/
├── README.md                    # what lives here, generated index of members
├── members.yaml                 # the manifest — who the collaborators are
├── meeting-notes/
│   ├── README.md                # GENERATED index, like hq/product/*
│   └── MO-26-08-03-09.30.00-cloudflare-migration.md
├── chat/
│   ├── 2026-08.md               # one file per month, append-only
│   └── assets.jsonl             # manifest of everything shared; blobs live in GCS
└── inbox/                       # ← eventually; see "what I would not do yet"
```

**Why `hq/team/` and not the alternatives you floated:**

- **`hq/ops/`** — already taken and means something else: legal, vendors, procurement (§5). Ops is
  administrative artefacts about running the company; a design argument in a group chat is not that.
- **Renaming `hq/inbox/` → `hq/team/`** — the right end state, wrong first move. See below.
- **A top-level `meeting-notes/`** — puts a medium at the same level as `apps/` and `hq/`, which
  says it is a pillar of the project rather than a record about it.

`team` is honest: it is the folder about the people and what passed between them.

### The team manifest — `hq/team/members.yaml`

```yaml
members:
  - github: cpheinrich
    name: Christopher Heinrich
    role: founder
    context: |
      Decides brand, spending, and anything with a legal surface. Prefers options
      over open questions. Reads on a phone as often as a laptop.
    channels:
      imessage: "+1..."        # optional, for attributing chat messages
```

Three things it must do, and one it must not:

- **Resolve an identity across systems.** A GitHub handle, a real name, and a phone number are the
  same person; without this, chat attribution and inbox ownership cannot be joined.
- **Validate.** `github` is checked against the handle rule already in `src/inbox/schema.ts`, so
  `hq/inbox/<handle>.md` and a member entry cannot drift.
- **Carry `context` as free prose.** Your "member-context" idea is the highest-value field here and
  the only one an agent cannot derive. It belongs in the standing explainer a voice session gets,
  and in a reviewer's understanding of who wrote a PR.
- **It must not become an access-control list.** `morpheus.json`'s `hq.allowlist` already does that
  and is enforced against Firebase claims (§13). Two lists of people, one of which is load-bearing
  for auth, is how someone gets access by editing the wrong file.

### Meeting notes

Filename and id follow the roadmap convention — `PREFIX-YY-MM-DD-HH.MM.SS-slug.md` — with **one
deliberate difference:**

> **The timestamp is when the meeting happened, not when the file was written.**

Roadmap ids come from the clock at creation because nothing better exists. A meeting has a real
event time, and using file-creation time would make ids sort in the order someone got round to
writing notes rather than the order things were said. Same format, different source; worth stating
because it is exactly the kind of unstated difference that rots later.

```yaml
---
id: MO-26-08-03-09.30.00
title: "Cloudflare account consolidation"
kind: meeting
occurred: 2026-08-03T09:30:00-07:00
duration_minutes: 45
attendees: [cpheinrich, alex]        # must resolve against members.yaml
source: granola                       # granola | manual | otter | …
source_ref: "grn_01H..."             # so the transcript can be re-fetched
decisions: 2                          # counts, for the index
actions: 3
---

## Summary
## Decisions
## Action items
## Notes
```

**`decisions` and `actions` are structured, not prose**, because they are the edges out of this
document. An action item should become a roadmap item with one command; a decision should promote to
`.agent/decisions.md`. A meeting note whose outputs cannot be traversed is an archive, and MO-048's
rule applies: *draw an edge when the schema declares it and nothing traverses it* — so do not declare
these fields until the traversal is built alongside them.

**Ingestion already exists for this one.** The Granola MCP connector is configured on this machine
and exposes `list_meetings` and `get_meeting_transcript`. So the first source needs an adapter, not
an integration.

### Group chat

**One file per month, append-only** — `hq/team/chat/2026-08.md`. Not one file, because a single
growing log means every sync rewrites one file, every diff is enormous, and `git log` becomes chat.
Monthly chunks keep diffs bounded and confine a bad sync to one file.

**Assets: content-addressed, manifest in git, blobs in GCS.**

```jsonl
{"sha256":"a1b2…","name":"dns-before.png","mime":"image/png","bytes":184320,
 "sent_by":"cpheinrich","sent_at":"2026-08-03T09:34:11-07:00",
 "bucket":"gs://morpheus-chat-assets/a1/b2/a1b2…","caption":"current DNS records"}
```

Content-addressing rather than sequential names, so the manifest is stable, duplicates collapse, and
**a missing asset is detectable** — the repo can assert that a blob it references still exists,
which a sequential name cannot.

## Three things I would decide before writing any code

**1. This is a privacy decision before it is a storage one.** Morpheus is a public repo. A group
chat contains other people's words, and git is append-only in practice — a message someone asks you
to delete cannot really be removed once pushed, only rewritten out of history everyone has cloned.

So: chat capture must be **opt-in per project**, refuse to run in a public repo without an explicit
override, and everyone in the chat should know it is archived. I would put that in `decisions.md`
before the first line of adapter code, not after.

**2. "One group chat per project" will not hold.** iMessage threads follow people, not repos — one
thread will span two projects, and some projects will have none. Better to have the project
*declare* its source in `morpheus.json` (`team.chat.source`) and let a thread map to zero or one
projects, than to assume a correspondence that reality will break within a month.

**3. Capture is worthless without a decay path.** In a year this is tens of thousands of chat lines.
Nothing in the current design says what an agent should read, and "everything" is the answer that
makes context worse. The distillation edges above are not a nice-to-have; they are the thing that
makes the raw capture affordable.

## What I would not do yet

**Do not move `hq/inbox/` into `hq/team/`.** It is the right end state and it is a migration across
six repos plus `inbox validate`, `morpheus init`, `check pr`'s records paths, `src/paths.ts`, the
tests, and every archive reference. Build `hq/team/` alongside it; move the inbox once there is
something else in the folder worth being adjacent to. *Extract on the second use* applies to
directory structure too.

**Do not scaffold this into every project.** Morpheus is `kind: internal` and has no team beyond
one person. `doctor` must not report a missing `hq/team/` as drift, and `init` should create it only
where a project declares collaborators.

## Suggested order

1. `members.yaml` + schema + validation — smallest, and everything else references it.
2. Meeting notes + Granola adapter — highest value, and ingestion already exists.
3. The decision and action-item edges out of a meeting note.
4. Chat capture, only after the privacy decision is recorded.
5. Asset manifest and GCS.

## Open questions

- **Does `hq/team/` land, or does `hq/inbox/` become `hq/team/` now?** The migration cost is real
  and I would defer it; your call.
- **Which repos get chat capture?** All private ones by default, or explicitly per project.
- **Do agents belong in `members.yaml`?** They are collaborators, they write inbox items, and the
  inbox already tags them. I lean no — a roster of humans that also contains `claude` makes
  "who is on this project" ambiguous — but it is arguable.
