## Context

Chris required the delivery agent and shared DevOps persona to document a candidate blocker,
why it is hard, and the reasonable alternatives tried with their outcomes before asking him to
intervene. Generic upload errors and obsolete internal funding holds had stalled recovery even
though authorized diagnostic and repair options remained.

## Approach

Extend the existing CTO mandate and DevOps incident guide with a compact blocker record and a
requirement to exhaust reasonable authorized options. Respect the newest engagement spending
policy, including explicit spend-as-needed instructions, while preserving actual caps, review,
credential and uncertain-effect safeguards. Distribute through canonical skills, Claude mirrors
and the existing generated CLI guide; no new supervisor, queue or blocker framework.

## Acceptance

- A candidate hard blocker records the symptom/evidence, why progress requires an external
  action or limit, attempts and outcomes, remaining options, and the minimal required action.
- A reasonable authorized recovery option keeps work in diagnosis/repair, with no fixed attempt
  count or requirement for a human to suggest it.
- A generic upload exception, an obsolete reservation counter, a confirmed missing credential,
  and a genuinely exhausted budget receive appropriately different treatment.
- The guide honors explicit engagement spending policy without faking settlements, increasing
  a cap, widening credentials or allowing uncertain upload replay.
- Both provider mirrors and installed CLI guide content remain consistent; focused distribution
  tests, skill validation, independent review and required CI pass.
