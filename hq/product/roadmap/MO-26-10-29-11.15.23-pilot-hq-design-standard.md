## Context

The shared standard needs real cross-project evidence before a broad corpus migration. The audit
identified one dense-data canary, one long operational-procedure canary and strong compact
exemplars, plus unresolved duplication between Evo's own `/hq` and Darwin's canonical Evo HQ.

## Approach

After the shared shell and data primitives are review-ready, file and coordinate project-owned
pilots: Lakina EXP-031 for the dense table/report shell; Darwin's nutrition collection instruction
for executive ordering, procedure hierarchy and appendices; and Darwin's Evo hub/algorithms record
as the compact reference. Resolve the canonical ownership/name of Evo HQ before migrating both
surfaces. Preserve source meaning and stable URLs, capture final builds at desktop/tablet/mobile,
and use the resulting evidence to revise the standard before inventory-driven migration of active
pages and then archives.

Darwin's expanded pilot showed that hierarchy and component reuse are not sufficient when the
prose itself remains expensive to scan. The standard now adds a measurable brevity contract:
current direction is a verb-led outcome of at most ten words; an executive brief is at most 75
words or five one-sentence bullets; and bloated human-facing legacy pages should usually lose
40–60% of their prose while retaining decisions, actions, owners, deadlines, thresholds, evidence,
caveats and compliance constraints. Legal/compliance documents and agent-centered records are
explicitly exempt from that compression target. Both independent-review personas enforce the same
contract, with ordinary copy preferences remaining non-blocking notes.
